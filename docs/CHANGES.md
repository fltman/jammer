# Changes that someone could notice

Not a list of commits: what behaves differently from before, for a user, an agent, or a file. Newest first.

## 2026-11-19

- **A sheet before every model download**: Settings › Models' Download, and Find Speakers the first time, show the model's size, publisher, exact source and version, license and place before anything is fetched. An agent's `diarize` (or a batch) no longer downloads the model: it answers `model_missing` with `"requirement": "diarisation"`.
- **What Jämmer may need installed**: one register for the six models and the plugin kinds, with each checksum or commit matched against what the source publishes (KB-Whisper and MRX are now fetched at a fixed commit instead of the newest). The agent guide has the section, `GET /api/v1/requirements` the list, and `POST /api/v1/settings/open` opens Settings at Models.
- **Speech made with a cloned voice keeps its mark**: read from a file's comment on import, kept through cuts, mixes and repairs, written into every file exported or sent to Flimmer, shown on the track as "Cloned voice" and in the API; it can't be removed.
- **Voice profiles** (on this Mac only, with consent): save a found speaker's voice under a name, and later recordings name speakers who sound like it ("is like", with the likeness). Settings › Voice Profiles lists and deletes them; the files are readable only by you. **Split Speakers onto Tracks** in the Audio menu.
- **Omnibar**, as in Glimmer: a glass field at the bottom of the window, where ⌘K puts the keyboard. It searches the commands as the palette did, with the ones you use first, and sends a request to the assistant (⌘Return always does). A text that could be either is decided by Apple's model on this Mac. The palette sheet is gone; **Edit › Omnibar** takes its place. Manual chapter 8.
- **Tracks by name**: a step can name a track, bus or group by its name, ignoring case, as well as by its id; a name that fits none or several fails with the list of them. A failure is said once ("duck: …", not "duck: duck: …"). The assistant on the Mac's model: 96 of 118 on the six evaluation sets, the same before and after; "lower the music under speech" still doesn't go through on it (manual chapter 14).
- **Send to Flimmer in every build**, after the last live run against Flimmer's guest passed (J3, pass 11: a 10-minute line in two parts, transcripts sent twice, a send canceled while preparing and one canceled 0.2 s after Send, rolled back in Flimmer with nothing left). Manual chapter 19, "Sending the sound back to Flimmer".
- **Send to Flimmer**: a cancel reaches Flimmer's job whenever it comes, and a cancel that came after Flimmer had finished says so (the clips are remembered, and the change is undone in Flimmer with ⌘Z).

## 2026-11-18

- **Automation in every build**: lanes for level, pan, mute, sends, place, a field's turn and a plugin's parameters, drawn by hand in the timeline (⌥-click, drag, the keys) or written from the Mixer's faders (Read, Touch, Latch). Anders tried it in the debug build. Manual chapter 24.
- **Plugin settings from elsewhere load only out of process**: "Load in Separate Processes" runs such an Audio Unit in a process of its own.
- **Transcripts to Flimmer**, with Send to Flimmer and on their own to the clips sent before; a canceled send cancels Flimmer's job too. Tried live against Flimmer's guest copy.

- **A linked document with changes survives a crash by Jämmer's own journal** (the-apps/SIBLINGS.md §6, point 6, as rewritten 2026-11-17): a record a document in the data folder (`Link Journal`), written when the document is linked and after every step, whole or not at all, off the main thread. At start, every record with changes that were not saved back is opened again, linked and with the changes, whatever the system's saved windows say; the window says "Recovered: not saved back yet", and `linkedFile` has `recovered: true` (after an unexpected stop) or `kept: true` (after a quit or a window closed with the work kept), never both, and `unreturnedSteps`, until the changes are saved back or given up. A linked window is no longer among the system's saved windows and has no autosave of its own: one way back, not two. A clean linked document leaves no record at a clean quit. A record that can't be read is left where it is and counted (`linkJournal.broken` in `GET /api/v1/status`).

  - What a record costs, measured: a two-hour project with a 20 000-word transcript and 600 clips is 4.1 MB and written in 44 ms (debug build, a loaded Mac), on a queue of its own; a step is not held up by it. The record carries the changes, not the sound.
  - Jämmer is not sandboxed: the link's bookmark is an ordinary one, with no security scope to open.
  - Not carried: the undo history from before the stop.
  - **An ordinary unsaved document is not in the journal** and is not meant to be: macOS's autosave is its protection, and it goes by the clock. What a crash costs there today is what was done since the last autosave; in the test above, a change made 3 to 5 s before the hard stop was gone. How long the interval is at most was not measured.
  - **Quit never asks about a linked document** (the series' way): its changes stay in the journal and the document opens again at the next start, "Kept from last time: not saved back yet"; undo starts from there. **Closing the window** with changes asks Save Back, Keep for Later, Discard or Cancel, and says how many steps wait. **File › Linked Files Waiting…** lists kept work (file, app, steps, age) with Open and Discard…; Discard asks once, says what is lost, and moves the record to the Trash. The same file handed over again opens the kept work. Tried in the app, each of them.
  - **Measured for an ordinary unsaved document, before** (one Mac, the system's saved windows on): a hard stop 6 s after a change lost the document; 20 s after, it came back with the change. So autosave landed somewhere between 6 and 20 s, and until its first autosave a new document wasn't protected at all.
  - **Now an ordinary document is autosaved a second after each step** (steps within the second make one write), instead of by the clock. Tried: a hard stop 2 s and 4 s after a change in a document that had never been saved, and it came back with the change both times. Playback isn't disturbed: a two-hour document (350 clips, 600 markers) played while 12 steps were made 1.6 s apart, each autosaved, with 0 underruns before and after. Jämmer doesn't record, so there is no recording to disturb. **The autosave is written on a thread of its own**: what it will write is taken on the main thread (a value; nothing is copied) and coded elsewhere. Measured with a two-hour project of 600 clips and a 20 000-word transcript, playing, six steps: each autosave held the main thread 0.01 to 0.03 ms and was written in about 160 ms on its own thread; 0 underruns. (The first build of it crashed on the first autosave: the document's write was still held to the main thread by its declaration. The check with the long project found it.)
  - **Found by the same check, and put right:** a step in that long document took about a second to answer (0.9 to 1.1 s for a fader change). Three things grew with the transcript, none of them the step itself (0.3 ms): the transcript panel built its whole text again at every step, 20 000 words, about 250 ms; the history coded the whole transcript again at every step to find that it hadn't changed, 23 ms; and the note of which media a document uses, which goes through every step of the history, ran after every autosave (35 ms and growing with the history; that one came with the autosave after each step). Now the panel builds its text only when what it shows has changed, the history keeps the last transcript's key, and the note is made at a real save and when a step brings a new file. Measured after: 16 to 27 ms for the same step from request to answer (the target set beforehand was 50 ms), 3 ms of it taking the step into the document.
  - **A quit that is called off** (tried): with a linked document and an ordinary one both changed, Quit shows the system's own question about unsaved documents (Review Changes…, Discard Changes, Cancel), which counts the linked one too; after Cancel both documents are as they were, and closing the linked window then asks its own question as usual. Whatever is answered there, the linked document's changes stay in the journal when Jämmer does quit: answered Discard Changes (tried), Jämmer quit, and at the next start the linked document was back with its change, "Kept from last time".

- **File › Storage…**, the first place where Jämmer moves files away, built to the rule that the user's data is never removed:
  - Only files Jämmer made itself in its media folder (effects, repairs, frozen strips, stems) and noted in a register there (`.jammer-media-register.json`, written whole, in the folder so it follows it) can be offered. A file without a row is an original and is never touched; that includes derived files from before the register, which are shown in a group of their own with their sizes. Decoded or resampled copies of imported files are treated as originals. A register that is missing or can't be read means nothing can be chosen, and a broken one is not written over.
  - "No longer used" means: no project that can be read uses it now or at any step of its history, on any branch, and no project that has used it is out of reach. Known projects are the open ones, the recent ones, autosaved ones, and those the register has seen use a file. A project that can't be read makes its files "can't tell", not free. Only an untitled document that is no longer open, and an autosaved one whose file is gone, are taken as gone for certain.
  - A document's use of a file is noted when it opens, when it is saved, and when a step makes it a file, not only when someone looks.
  - Nothing happens by itself. The window shows the groups with sizes; the user ticks files; a question says how many and how much and that they stay in the Trash until it is emptied; the files are moved to the Trash (`FileManager.trashItem`), never deleted; afterwards the window says so. What is to be moved is worked out first and then moved from the same reading.
  - A project that needs a file that was moved is told so in its missing-file warning, with the date.
  - `GET /api/v1/storage` reads the same. There is no call that removes anything (POST and DELETE answer 405).
  - Tested (`MediaStorageTests`): a project's present and a branch of its history keep a file in use; a project out of reach makes its file unknown and seen again frees it; files without a row are never offered; a request that names anything not freeable moves nothing at all; a dry run and the real move name the same files; a broken register. In the app, in a test copy with its own data folder: an echo that was undone stayed "in use" through the history; a planted file with no users was chosen, asked about ("Move 1 file to the Trash? That frees 20 KB."), moved, and the rest was untouched; the planted file of an out-of-reach project stayed "can't tell". One 20 KB file of zeros named "ZZ000001 echo.wav" is in the Trash from that run.
  - Not tried: a project on a disk that is really disconnected and then connected again; an autosaved untitled document after a crash; the missing-file hint seen in a window.

- **The plugin scanner could call a good plugin invalid on a busy Mac**, and the catalogue kept that until the bundle changed. Cause: the helper's answer was read both by a background handler and by the caller, and the verdict could be taken between the handler's read and its storing ("0 bytes out, exit 0"). Shown by slowing the handler (6 of 6 probes of a good plugin "invalid"); not reproduced by load alone in 60 probes. Now one reader per pipe and the verdict waits for both (the same slowing: 6 of 6 found; 80 of 80 under load).
  - Verdicts are kept apart: `invalid` only when the helper itself says the bundle isn't a plugin; no answer at all is `scan_failed` (new); `timed_out` as before. `timed_out` and `scan_failed` are tried again at every scan A bundle that has run out of time two scans running is then tried only at every fifth scan, or when a scan is forced (the Plugins sheet's scan button, `force` in the API), so a plugin that really hangs doesn't cost its time limit at every start; the sheet names it.
  - A plugin an earlier scan found stays listed through a failed scan, with `kept: true` in `scan.failed`; a crash or "not a plugin" replaces it only when it comes twice running.
